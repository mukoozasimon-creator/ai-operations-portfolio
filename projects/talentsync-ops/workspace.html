Four proposed questions for each draft

plain-English checks
Is it true?
Check names, dates and numbers against their source records. Hold unsupported details for review; a matching source can still contain an error.
Check unsupported details
Is it allowed?
Check consent, approved data use and agreed restrictions. Hold suspected rule breaches for a person to resolve.
Check agreed limits
Is it complete?
Check for missing names, incomplete sentences and broken links before asking a person to approve the draft.
Check missing information
Is it safe?
Flag sensitive or unusual content for a person to assess. Automated checks alone cannot guarantee safety.
Flag uncertainty

Three levels of checking - matched to the risk

how the gate scales

A proposed pilot could combine rule checks, an additional AI review and human approval according to risk. These are design choices to test, not operating controls in this preview.

LevelWhat it doesUsed for
Auto-checkedConfigured field and rule checksEvery single draft
AI-reviewedA second AI read of the wording and reasoningAnything member-facing
Human-approvedA named person reviews the draft before sendingAnything that leaves the network

When we choose not to use AI

judgement, not reflex

Use a rule for a predictable task, and consider AI when interpreting varied text would help. Either approach needs testing, error handling and clear ownership.

Just needs a rule
A scheduled rule could prepare the weekly summary. It would still need checks for missing records, connection failures and delivery rules.
Proposed rule-based flows
Worth using AI
An AI assistant could help draft a reply to a varied member note. A person would still review the context, wording and proposed action.

Users and Access - the trust ladder

These example roles illustrate a possible onboarding path. This public preview has no sign-in or role enforcement. A live system would need agreed permissions, training and tested access controls.

Step 1 - Sandbox
Explore sample data
A new user could practise with fictional records before working with live data. This preview shows fixed examples.
Step 2 - Graduation
Review readiness together
A future pilot could agree practice tasks and a review with the owner before granting access. No training progress is assessed here.
Step 3 - Live pilot
Role-based access
A live system would restrict access by role and record sensitive-data access. Those controls are proposed, not implemented here.
